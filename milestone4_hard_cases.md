# Milestone 4 — Hard Cases

Posts that made us pause during labeling, which two labels they could have gone to, and what was decided.

## 1. "I listened to the dramatized audiobooks at work so I was pretty entertained if only for the dragons."

**Could have been:** `analysis` or `take`

**Chose:** `take`, because "if only for the dragons" is a soft attribution, not an explicit causal link ("because," "that's why"). It names a detail (dragons) next to the verdict (entertained) without building an actual argument from it. This is the same shape as the original Dewey boundary case from the taxonomy, just with weaker phrasing — a good test of how strict the "explicit reasoning language" rule really is.

## 2. "No, it actually gets worse. That's why it's such a popular, beloved and rewatched show."

**Could have been:** `analysis` or `take`

**Chose:** `take`. This is sarcasm — the literal words say "gets worse," but the intended meaning is the opposite (it gets much better). Even reading past the sarcasm to the real claim, there's no specific, checkable detail behind it — just a general appeal to popularity. Sarcasm turned out to be a recurring wrinkle: the label has to track the *actual* claim being made, not the literal sentence, but that claim still has to clear the same bar for evidence.

## 3. "Bojack Horseman did a great job developing characters."

**Could have been:** `analysis` or `take`

**Chose:** `take`, but it sits right next to several comments in the same thread that *were* labeled `analysis` — e.g. "Rick becomes a little more understanding and selfless. Morty learns confidence..." This pair clarified the real rule being applied: naming a show and asserting a verdict isn't enough on its own. `analysis` requires describing the actual trajectory or mechanism (what changed, from what, to what), not just a conclusory label like "did a great job."

## 4. "Jason has always been a victim. Just like his mother went crazy from losing her son he went crazy from losing his mother... Jason was always a pretty sympathetic villain to a degree."

**Could have been:** in scope (as `analysis`) or out of scope entirely

**Chose:** excluded. This reads as franchise-wide character lore/analysis (discussing the Jason Voorhees character across the whole Friday the 13th series) rather than a judgment about the new Crystal Lake show's execution specifically. The inclusion rule is about evaluating *this show*, not the character's history in general — a distinction that wasn't obvious until this post showed up.

## 5. "This show was INSANELY popular in those [QAnon] groups, which has honestly completely turned me off of watching it."

**Could have been:** in scope (as `analysis`) or excluded as off-topic

**Chose:** included, as `analysis`. It mechanically fits the rule (a specific fact — the show's popularity in a particular group — explicitly causing a stated verdict about watching it), but conceptually it's closer to the "about a person/real life" exclusion from Milestone 1 than to a judgment about the show's content. Kept in because it's still about engaging with *this show*, not an unrelated real-world event, but flagged as the closest call to that boundary.
