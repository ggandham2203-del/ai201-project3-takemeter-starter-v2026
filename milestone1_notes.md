# Milestone 1 — Community and Candidate Distinctions

## Community

**r/television** — general TV discussion subreddit (not a single-show community). Public, no login required.

## Reading

Read through comments across multiple threads, including:
- "What are you watching and what do you recommend" (general check-in thread)
- "Malcolm in the Middle is the show that has aged..." (nostalgia/quality discussion)
- "Breaking Bad star RJ Mittes heartbreaking..." (actor news thread)
- "What is Disney even doing with the Muppets now" (show-decline discussion)
- "What's an episode of a TV show that made you go nope" (specific-episode reactions)

Read more than the examples quoted below; these are the comments that most clearly illustrated each pattern.

## Candidate Distinctions

**1. Argued vs. asserted**
Some comments back a claim with a specific, checkable detail. Others state a verdict with no support.
- Argued: "the 2011 [Muppets] movie did well at the box office, but the sequel and the attempts at tv shows don't find a massive audience like other IPs"
- Asserted: "Malcolm in the Middle has aged really well"

**2. About the show vs. about a person/real life**
Some comments evaluate the show itself. Others are personal disclosure or reaction to real-world news about someone involved, unrelated to the show's quality.
- About the show: "I love the Big Tech spin to what would otherwise be a standard legal procedural."
- About a person: "I was suicidal this year for the first time in my life."

**3. Opinion vs. pure status update**
Some comments carry an evaluative stance. Others just report what someone is watching, with no judgment attached.
- Status update: "Just started The Sopranos for the first time last night"
- Opinion: "I love the Big Tech spin to what would otherwise be a standard legal procedural."

**4. Named/specific vs. vague**
Some comments anchor to an exact scene, episode, or stat. Others use floating descriptors with nothing to point at.
- Named/specific: "that junkyard town with the lady with the bad haircut. I think they were called the Scavengers"
- Vague: "aged beautifully," "timeless"

## Scoping decision

Posts reacting to real-world news about a person (distinction #2) will be **excluded** from the 200-post dataset. They're an on-topic/off-topic split, not a quality-of-take distinction, and building a label around them would likely be thin and inconsistent with the rest of the taxonomy. Collection will focus on threads where people are evaluating shows/episodes, not reacting to real-world events involving cast members.
